
# Organic aerosol scenario

This scenario is the test bed for the organic aerosol scheme of MOSAIC: every formation pathway of the scheme has emissions, and the processing program writes the organic aerosol diagnostics. It is independent of the other scenarios.

The case that is set up here is an urban plume. It takes the meteorology, the initial and background conditions, the emissions of inorganic gases and CBM-Z organic gases and the particle size distributions from the `1_urban_plume` scenario and adds the emissions of the precursors of the organic aerosol. Other cases (chamber experiments, biogenic or biomass-burning environments) can be set up in the same way by replacing the input files.

The organic aerosol is that of the CRACMM3 mechanism of CMAQ 6.0 (Pye et al., 2023; Skipper et al., 2024): 24 semivolatile species (reactive organic carbon in volatility bins, alkane-like `AROCN2ALK` to `AROCP3ALK` and oxygenated `AROCN2OXY2` to `AROCP3OXY2`; the highly oxygenated molecules of the monoterpenes `AHOM` and `AELHOM`; the monoterpene nitrates `ATRPN` and `AHONIT`; the photolysis product of the HOM `AMTN1`; the aromatic peroxides `AOP3`) and 7 nonvolatile species (`AISO3NOS` and `AISO3OS` from the acid-catalyzed uptake of the isoprene epoxydiols, `AISO4` and `AISO5` from the uptake of the isoprene tetrafunctional compounds and nitrates, `AGLYOLIG` from the uptake of methylglyoxal, `ASOAT`, which the reactions of phenols, cresols and a few other gases form directly, and `AORGC` from cloud processing, which is not formed here). The 92 gas species of this organic aerosol block (the vapors of the semivolatile species, the precursors such as `SOATOL`, `SOAXYL`, `BENZ`, `NAPH`, `HC10`, `SESQ` and the IVOCs, and their radicals and products) react as tracers on the CBM-Z gas phase: their reactions read OH, HO2, NO, NO2, NO3, O3, CH3O2, C2O3 and `ISOPP` of CBM-Z but do not change them, and the reactions of the host species `API`, `LIM`, `BPIN`, `ISOP`, `ISOPP` and `ISPX` that form block species run at the rates of the host reactions. The species, the reactions and the properties of the aerosol species (C* at 298 K, enthalpy of vaporization, O:C and OM:OC; kappa = 0.11 OM:OC - 0.10 as in CMAQ, Pye et al., 2017) are generated from the mechanism file of CMAQ (`mech_cracmm3.def`) and its aerosol definitions (`ORG_DEFN.F`). In the particles, `ATRPN` and `AHONIT` hydrolyze to `AHOM` and nitric acid (lifetime 3 h) and `AHOM` photolyzes to 0.8 `AMTN1` and 0.2 formic acid at 1% of the photolysis frequency of NO2. The hydroxy hydroperoxides `ISHP` and the epoxydiols `IEPOX` follow CRACMM3 as species of the block: `ISHP` is formed at the rate of `ISOPP` + HO2 of CBM-Z and reacts with OH at the CRACMM3 rate constants to 0.44 `IEPOX` and 0.14 `IPX` (RAM07); `IEPOX` is also formed from the nitrates `ISON` (RAM10) and lost by OH (RAM17). The epoxydiols are taken up as in CMAQ by particles with an aqueous phase and form `AISO3OS` in the fraction that reacts with sulfate, taking one mole of sulfate out of the inorganic sulfate of the particle per mole, as in CMAQ (the rest forms `AISO3NOS`); `IPX` and `INALD` are taken up at 2 and 0.5 times the rate of the epoxydiols, `INALD` releasing nitric acid. The `ASOAT` that the gas-phase reactions form is counted in the gas species `PROD_ASOAT` and moved to the particles in every time step (CMAQ adds it to its accumulation mode), distributed over them as the condensation of a nonvolatile vapor of 200 g/mol.

In the gas phase, the reactions of the monoterpenes (`API`, `LIM`, `BPIN`) with OH, O3 and NO3 consume the oxidant and form the gas-phase products of the monoterpene reactions of CB6r5 (TERP and APIN in `cb6r5_ae7_aq`), written with the species of CBM-Z. In the MOSAIC library of 2012 these were tracer reactions: OH was not consumed and no radicals were formed, so that OH was too high wherever monoterpenes are an important sink of it. One gas species was added for this, `XO2N`, the organic peroxy radical that forms an organic nitrate with NO instead of NO2. A `gas_data.dat` without `XO2N` still works, but this radical is then lost at the end of every time step.

The isoprene peroxy radical (`ISOPP`) also follows CB6r5: its reaction with HO2 forms the hydroxy hydroperoxides `ISPX` with 0.12 OH and HO2 (in CBM-Z it formed the generic hydroperoxide `ROOH`), and it isomerizes to HO2 and the hydroperoxy aldehydes, the gas species `HPLD`, which photolyze to OH at 0.055 times the photolysis frequency of NO2. The reaction of `ISPX` with OH consumes OH and returns 0.933 OH; the epoxydiols that it forms in CB6r5 are not followed here (see the organic aerosol above). Under low NO these reactions return most of the radicals that the oxidation of isoprene consumed in CBM-Z. The three isoprene peroxy radicals (`ISOPP`, `ISOPN`, `ISOPO2`) react with NO at 2.7e-12 exp(350/T) cm3 s-1 and `ISOPP` reacts with HO2 at 2.12e-13 exp(1300/T) cm3 s-1, the measured rate constants (Wennberg et al., Chem. Rev. 118, 3337, 2018), instead of the CBM-Z values (4.0e-12 and 1.7e-13 exp(1300/T)); together with the isomerization these set how much of the isoprene ends as hydroxy hydroperoxides. A `gas_data.dat` needs `ISPX` and `HPLD`: without them the products of `ISOPP` + HO2 and of the isomerization are lost at the end of every time step.

Two photolysis frequencies also follow CB6r5 (ratios to that of NO2, from the cross sections and quantum yields of `cb6r5_ae7_aq`): methylglyoxal `MGLY` 1.41e-2 and the organic nitrates `ONIT` 3.2e-4. The CBM-Z values were 2.0 and 0.31 times these. In an urban plume the slower photolysis of `MGLY` lowers HO2 by about a tenth and the ozone maximum by a few percent; the organic aerosol does not change beyond the noise of the particle sampling, except for the SOA from methylglyoxal, which follows its gas concentration. The isoprene products `ISOPRD` (methacrolein, methyl vinyl ketone) photolyze at 3.67e-4 times the frequency of NO2: the cross sections of CMAQ (the mean of the two molecules) with the quantum yields recommended by the JPL evaluation 19-5 (Burkholder et al., 2020; measured by Gierczak et al., 1997), exp(-0.055 (lambda - 308 nm)) / (5.5 + 9.2e-19 [M]) for methyl vinyl ketone and 0.005 for methacrolein (recommended: below 0.01). CMAQ itself uses a quantum yield of 0.05 at all wavelengths for this species, an upper limit from an earlier study, which gives 2.87e-3 and would make this photolysis a tenth of the primary source of radicals where isoprene dominates; the CBM-Z value was 1.48e-4. The photolysis of `ISOPRD` also has the products of CB6r5 (R161), written with the species of CBM-Z: 1.10 HO2 + 0.50 XO2 + 0.34 CH3O2 + 0.208 C2O3 + 0.26 HCHO + 0.24 OLET + 0.24 PAR + 0.17 AONE + 0.128 ALD2, where ALD2 stands for the glycolaldehyde of CB6r5; CBM-Z formed 0.97 C2O3 and 0.7 CH3O2, that is more peroxyacetyl nitrate and less HO2.

Three further reactions of the radicals follow CMAQ. The peroxyacetyl radical reacts with HO2 as in CB6r5 (R57: 3.14e-12 exp(580/T) cm3 s-1, 0.5 OH + 0.5 CH3O2 + 0.13 O3 + 0.5 `RCOOH`); in CBM-Z this reaction ended the radical chain (0.4 O3 + 0.4 `RCOOH`). The reaction of the isoprene peroxy radical `ISOPP` with the other organic peroxy radicals forms 0.8 HO2, 0.598 HCHO and 0.072 `XO2` besides `ISOPRD` (CB6r5 R154); in CBM-Z it formed `ISOPRD` only. O(1D) is quenched by O2 and N2 at 3.3e-11 exp(55/T) and 2.15e-11 exp(110/T) cm3 s-1 (the values of the CRACMM3 mechanism of CMAQ 6.0, whose sum for air is the O1D + M of CB6r5) and reacts with water at 2.14e-10 cm3 s-1 (CB6r5 R11), so that an O(1D) atom forms 12% less OH than with the rate constants of CBM-Z. Together these lower HO2 by 10 to 15% and the ozone maximum by 4% in the urban plume of this scenario; where isoprene dominates, OH is 3% lower in the afternoon.

HO2 is lost on the particles as in the CRACMM3 mechanism of CMAQ 6.0 (reaction `HET_HO2`): without products, with the uptake coefficient 0.2 (Ivatt et al., 2022) and limited by gas-phase diffusion. PartMC computes the loss frequency of every time step from the wet diameter of each particle, k = sum of n pi D^2 / (D / (2 Dg) + 4 / (c gamma)), with the mean molecular speed c of HO2 and the gas-phase diffusivity Dg that CMAQ uses for its uptake reactions; CMAQ uses the wet surface area of its accumulation mode instead of the sum over all particles. The optional spec file line `ho2_uptake_coeff` (after `do_fixed_photolysis` and `iepox_acidity`) sets the uptake coefficient; the default is 0.2 and 0 switches the loss off. Uptake coefficients measured on ambient particles are 0.1 to 0.4, those on purely organic particles are ten times smaller (Taketani et al., 2012; Lakey et al., 2015). In the urban plume of this scenario, with 50 ug m-3 of particles, the loss lowers HO2 by 4 to 20% during the day and by half at night, and the ozone maximum by 5% (122 instead of 129 ppb).

## Emissions

The emission files `gas_emit.dat` and `aero_emit_comp_*.dat` are generated by `make_emissions.py` from `gas_emit_base.dat` (the gas emissions of `1_urban_plume`) and the particle emissions in `aero_emit_dist.dat`. All scaling factors are at the top of that script. To change them, edit the script and run `./make_emissions.py`.

| Species | Emission | Source of the scaling |
|---|---|---|
| `SOATOL` | 1 × `TOL` | Toluene of CRACMM3 (`TOL` there), a tracer with the CRACMM3 reactions; `TOL` of CBM-Z keeps its own chemistry. |
| `SOAXYL` | 0.998 × `XYL` | Xylenes of CRACMM3 (`XYL` there); xylene without naphthalene, see `NAPH`. |
| `NAPH` | 0.002 × `XYL` | CMAQv5.1 VOC emission updates (Pouliot and Pye), estimate for inventories without naphthalene, based on the 2011 NEI. |
| `HC10` | 0.108 × `PAR` | Same source, the estimate of the long-chain alkanes; in CRACMM3 the alkanes with an OH rate constant above 6.8e-12 cm3/s. |
| `BENZ` | 1.3e-3 × `CO` | Benzene (`BEN` of CRACMM3). Urban emission ratio of benzene to CO in Los Angeles, 1.30 pptv/ppbv (Borbon et al., 2013, Table 2; Paris: 1.07 at the ground, 2.50 airborne). |
| `API` | 0.0618 × `ISOP` | Global annual emissions of MEGAN2.1 (Guenther et al., 2012, Table 6): α-pinene 66.1 Tg, isoprene 535 Tg. |
| `BPIN` | 0.0214 × `ISOP` | Same table: β-pinene 18.9 Tg and camphene 4.0 Tg. β-pinene has its own species in CBM-Z because it reacts with ozone at a fifth of the rate of α-pinene (IUPAC rate constants); CRACMM3 forms the products of `API` from it. |
| `LIM` | 0.0685 × `ISOP` | Same table: all other monoterpenes, 73.3 Tg. |
| `SESQ` | 0.0181 × `ISOP` | Same table: all sesquiterpenes, 29.0 Tg. |
| `VROCP3ALK`, `VROCP4ALK`, `VROCP5ALK`, `VROCP6ALK`, `VROCP5ARO`, `VROCP6ARO` | 0.0032, 0.0034, 0.0019, 0.0064, 0.0060, 0.0253 × NMOG (mass) | Organic gases of intermediate volatility of gasoline exhaust (Lu et al., 2020), as in the emission control files of CMAQ for CRACMM (v5.4 `cracmm1_aq`, v5.5 `cracmm2`). | POA in particles | cooking: `AROCN2OXY2` 0.35, `AROCP0OXY2` 0.35, `AROCP1OXY1` 0.10, `AROCP2OXY2` 0.10, `AROCP3OXY2` 0.10; diesel: `AROCN1ALK` 0.187, `AROCP0ALK` 0.086, `AROCP1ALK` 0.364, `AROCP2ALK` 0.363; gasoline: `AROCN1ALK` 0.228, `AROCP0ALK` 0.108, `AROCP1ALK` 0.515, `AROCP2ALK` 0.149 (mass fractions of the POA) | Volatility profiles of the same CMAQ files: meat cooking (Woody et al., 2016; the default of CMAQ for sources without a profile of their own), diesel and gasoline exhaust (Lu et al., 2020). All ratios are molar unless noted otherwise.

Things to be aware of:

* The primary organic matter of the cooking, diesel and gasoline particles is emitted in the particle phase, as far as the POA mass of the inventory goes, with the volatility profiles above. It then evaporates according to the gas-particle partitioning, so most of it is in the gas phase shortly after emission. CMAQ instead emits the more volatile part of each profile directly as vapor; the total is the same.
* The size distributions and the organic mass fractions of the emitted particles are those of `1_urban_plume`. The POA species have a density of 1400 kg/m^3 and `OC` has 1000 kg/m^3, so the emitted organic mass is larger than in `1_urban_plume` (by 40% for the cooking particles, less for the others).
* The initial and background particles contain the nonvolatile `OC`, as in `1_urban_plume`.
* The terpene emissions follow the diurnal profile of isoprene. Real monoterpene emissions continue at night, so the nighttime chemistry with the nitrate radical is weaker here than in a forested environment.
* The global ratios of terpenes to isoprene are not specific to any region.

## Running

    ./1_run.sh
    ./2_process.sh
    ./3_plot.sh

`organics_process.F90` writes `out/organics_process.nc` with the time series (averaged over the repeats) of

* `mass_conc_<group>`: mass concentration of the species groups of CMAQ's species definitions for CRACMM3 (`SpecDef_Conc_cracmm3.txt`): `poa` (the alkane-like reactive organic carbon, APOM of CMAQ, and `OC`), `soa_roc` (the oxygenated reactive organic carbon, `AOP3` and `ASOAT`, AORGA of CMAQ: SOA from aromatics, alkanes, IVOCs and sesquiterpenes, but also the oxygenated POA of cooking, which is emitted as these species), `soa_monoterpene`, `soa_isoprene` (together AORGB of CMAQ), `soa_glyoxal` and `soa_cloud`,
* `tot_org_mass_conc`, `tot_sec_org_mass_conc`: all organic matter and secondary organic matter (everything except the group `poa`, as ASOM of CMAQ),
* `tot_o_c`, `tot_om_oc`: carbon-weighted O:C and OM:OC of all organic matter; `sec_o_c`: O:C of the secondary organic matter,

and one file per output time with the two-dimensional number distributions of dry diameter against the per-particle O:C (`diam_o_c_dist`) and against the secondary organic fraction of the dry mass (`diam_sec_frac_dist`).
